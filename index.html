Truth Profile

What share of what politicians say is actually true, measured the same way for everyone?

Every checkable factual claim in politicians' speeches, extracted in full and verified name-blind by adversarial AI judges against primary evidence. Each claim is published with the sample size, uncertainty, evidence, dissent, and a public dispute channel.

  • 1,590 claims from 16 speeches by 8 politicians across both parties
  • One public rubric drives every judging prompt and the methodology page
  • Published bias audit, including a party-label swap test
My part I designed the process: check every claim instead of cherry-picking, hide who said it from the judges, put all the rules in one public rubric, and test for bias by swapping party labels. Claude models do the extraction and judging under those rules; I review the results.

glp1genome

What does current research actually say about how my genes affect GLP-1 drugs?

Upload your existing 23andMe or AncestryDNA file and get an honest, personalized report on what peer-reviewed research says about your genetics and response to GLP-1 medications like Ozempic and Wegovy.

  • Interprets variants from a 2026 Nature study of 27,885 people
  • Discloses replication failures instead of overselling results
  • Accounts, file upload, and saved reports with Supabase
My part I read the studies and decided which genetic variants to include and how to word the results, including telling users when a finding failed to replicate.

Campus Assault Index

How do colleges compare when you count only the students who actually live on campus?

Reported sexual offenses at 1,159 U.S. residential four-year colleges, per 10,000 on-campus students, built from federal Clery Act data (2022–2024) and compared against the national median.

  • Python pipeline merges Department of Education Clery data with IPEDS enrollment
  • Subtracts online-only students so rates reflect who actually lives on campus
  • Flags small schools, one-year spikes, and zero-report colleges with large dorms

Reported numbers aren't the same as how often assaults happen: a higher rate can mean students feel safer reporting. The site has its own Underreporting section on this.

My part I chose the federal data sources and the measure (reports per 10,000 on-campus students), decided which colleges qualify and what gets flagged, and checked the outliers by hand.
